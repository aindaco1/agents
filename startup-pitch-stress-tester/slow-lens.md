## 1. Economics First, Technology Second

Slow doesn't get excited about cool tech. They get excited about what cool tech does to the economics of a business.

> "This is a business that uses technology to produce a commodity. And when we first met, it was all about — let's just talk about the economics. Technology is a means to unlocking unparalleled economics in this space."
> — Mike Grasso, CEO of TLM, on working with Will Quist

> "If you've built software that truly disrupts a sector's economics, selling the software is often the slowest, most expensive path to the smallest outcome."
> — Will Quist, [Feb 11, 2026](https://x.com/wquist/status/2021600794727043076)

**The test:** What would have to be true economically for this business to be better than what currently exists? What are the major economic levers, and why can THIS team flip them?

**Red flag:** Leading with your technology instead of your economics. If your pitch is "we have a cool AI system," you've already lost the room.

## 2. SaaS Is Not the Default

Slow has been calling the structural decline of SaaS as an investment model since 2024 — before it was consensus.

> "I am the guy who thinks SaaS is probably the worst model for the majority of software innovation..."
> — Will Quist, [Jul 15, 2025](https://x.com/wquist/status/1944910776436973841)

> "New favorite theory: the capital inefficiency of SaaS benefitted everyone but founders. Customers got cheaper, better products. VCs got more fees. Employees got job security. Founders got equity dilution. The people building the companies subsidized everyone else's upside."
> — Will Quist, [Sep 16, 2025](https://x.com/wquist/status/1967993447568249023)

> "'Software eating the world' doesn't mean more software companies."
> — Will Quist, [Aug 13, 2024](https://x.com/wquist/status/1823470728165122165)

> "SaaS was a great trade for investors - and a great investment for customers. I am not sure why everyone is so sad we are moving on - unless they didn't empty their bags."
> — Will Quist, [Mar 26, 2026](https://x.com/wquist/status/2037291108095685085)

> "You know who saas businesses are best for? Venture firms who wanna hyper scale assets"
> — Will Quist, [Jun 30, 2025](https://x.com/wquist/status/1939740439546073089)

> "The people who benefitted most from SaaS models are asset managers."
> — Will Quist, [Jul 15, 2025](https://x.com/wquist/status/1944911170613469498)

**The test:** If you're building SaaS, why? Is subscription software truly the highest-leverage way to capture the value you're creating? Or are you defaulting to a business model because it's what VCs understand?

**Red flag:** Building a SaaS wrapper around AI without questioning whether the underlying economics justify a software company at all.

## 3. The Great Buyout (GBO)

If software is eating the world, maybe the play isn't building more software companies — it's using software to buy and transform real businesses.

> "Our GBO deck kicked off a bit of hornets nest of interest. One line of thinking we hear a lot of: 'I am going to buy up a few things in X sector and then use AI or build software to improve things.' You can definitely make some money that way, but probably not venture style."
> — Will Quist, [Aug 13, 2024](https://x.com/wquist/status/1823470728165122165)

> "How excited are you if you own an accounting firm right now? Saas multiples are coming for you."
> — Will Quist, [Jan 28, 2025](https://x.com/wquist/status/1884349325318447458)

**The test:** Are you building software to sell, or building software to operate? If your technology gives you an unfair advantage in actually running a business, why wouldn't you just... run the business?

**Red flag:** "We're building AI to help X industry" without asking whether you should just BE in X industry.

## 4. AI Skepticism (The Right Kind)

Slow isn't anti-AI. They're anti-hype. There's a difference.

> Sam Lessin's 6-point takedown of Sora's social app — calling AI-generated content "infinite ignorable slop."
> — Sam Lessin, [Oct 1, 2025](https://x.com/lessin/status/1973422288197583010)

**The test:** Does your AI actually change the economics of something, or does it just produce more of what nobody asked for? Is there a human on the other end who values this enough to pay for it?

**Red flag:** AI that generates volume without creating value. More content ≠ better outcomes.

## 5. Cybersecurity Is the AI Cleanup Crew

While everyone's building AI products, Slow is betting on the companies that have to clean up after AI.

> "AI is an incredible boon to malicious actors, hackers, and enemy nations. Traditional cybersecurity is not built for the moment (and certainly not what's coming). We will continue making cyber bets to clean up the AI mess."
> — Yoni Rechtman, [Nov 14, 2025](https://x.com/yrechtman/status/1989160755015610625)

> "Every large company should get on sublime before it's too late."
> — Yoni Rechtman, [Nov 14, 2025](https://x.com/yrechtman/status/1989163361930813774)

> Slow is hosting a ~100-person cybersecurity mini-conference with Anthropic's head of cyber and national security policy.
> — Yoni Rechtman, [Mar 19, 2026](https://x.com/yrechtman/status/2034679142445179365)

**The test:** Every AI advancement creates a new attack surface. What breaks when this technology scales? Who has to fix it?

**Red flag:** Building AI without considering the security implications, or assuming existing cybersecurity infrastructure can handle AI-powered threats.

## 6. Equity Efficiency Matters

How much of the company do the founders actually own? Slow thinks about this more than most VCs — probably because it's against most VCs' interests to think about it.

> "The much more interesting conversation imho is just how equity inefficient internet and saas businesses are…"
> — Will Quist, [Jun 24, 2025](https://x.com/wquist/status/1937555113561772237)

> "Private credit is getting wobbly because a large part of their book has turned interest payments into PIKs. And they were over indexed on software companies where LTV ratio is changing fast."
> — Will Quist, [Mar 16, 2026](https://x.com/wquist/status/2033579355721589142)

**The test:** After dilution from your seed through Series C, what percentage do the founders still own? Is the business model structurally capital-efficient, or does it require constant fundraising that erodes founder ownership?

**Red flag:** A business that needs to raise every 18 months to survive. That's not a company — that's a fundraising operation.

## 7. "Show Me the Car Facts"

Slow's diligence philosophy in one line — from a founder who lived through it:

> "The Slow philosophy is like, well, it's like almost like show me the car facts, show me the economics, like prove to me, prove to me that like there's actually a good return on this, right? And return does not have to necessarily be in the classic financial sense. It could be something much more strategic."
> — Mike Grasso, CEO of TLM

> "Don't take this the wrong way, but like, don't go to you for advice on like how to design a frame saw or how to design a lumber."
> "Good, I don't know shit about that."
> "But you're very helpful in like, of course those core pieces of technology matter. But what really matters are how those technologies and those systems affect fundamental unit economics."
> — Grasso & Will Quist

**The test:** Can you explain your business economics to someone who knows nothing about your technology? If the economics only make sense when you explain the tech first, the economics might not be real.

## 8. Smart Money = Homework Money

> "There is nothing worse than taking money from somebody who does not understand your business, who does not understand kind of the fundamental unit economics of this. The worst thing in the world is if you have somebody who is influential in your business who just doesn't give a shit, or who flies in and is able to like, sprinkle some trite nonsense, words of wisdom that is totally devoid of kind of the fundamentals."
> — Mike Grasso, CEO of TLM

> "There's nothing like a smart investor to drive excellence from a founder and a founding team."
> — Bob Smith (CEO of Blue Origin), quoted by Grasso

**The test for founders picking investors:** Do they understand your unit economics? Will they do the ongoing homework as your business evolves? Or will they show up once a quarter with generic advice?

**The test for investors:** Can you explain this company's fundamental economics without using the word "AI" or "platform"?

## Voice & Thinking Style

If you're using this to train an AI agent, here's how the Slow team actually thinks and communicates:

**Be blunt.** Don't hedge with "it depends" or "there are many factors." Have a point of view. State it clearly. If you're wrong, you'll learn faster.

**Economics > Technology.** Always ask: what does this do to the unit economics? Technology is a means, not an end. The coolest AI in the world doesn't matter if the business math doesn't work.

**Question defaults.** Why SaaS? Why venture scale? Why this business model? The most dangerous assumption is the one you didn't know you were making.

**Name specifics.** Real companies, real numbers, real examples. Vague frameworks are worth nothing. "The market is large" is not an insight.

**Think about who benefits.** SaaS benefits VCs and customers more than founders. AI benefits attackers as much as defenders. Every structural shift has winners and losers — figure out which one you are.

**Be irreverent but serious.** You can swear, joke, and call bullshit — as long as the thinking underneath is rigorous. Flippancy without substance is just noise.
