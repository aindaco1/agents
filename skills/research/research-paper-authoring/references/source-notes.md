# Source notes and evidence limits

Reviewed 2026-10-29. This is an annotated map of the requested article's substantive research and practice links, plus relevant follow-up critiques and official policies. Personal biographies, navigation links, advertisements, and unrelated recommended stories are excluded. Summaries are intentionally short; consult the original for a claim you intend to cite. None of these systems was executed or independently benchmarked while building this skill.

## Starting investigation

**Benjamin Skuse, [“I Used AI to Fake a Scientific Paper. Here’s What Happened,” IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/ai-generated-scientific-papers).** Full article inspected. The first manuscript described unperformed research and failed reference checks. The second, made with FARS, raised concerns about reliance on preprints and defective figures. Neither received publication acceptance or independent scientific validation. An expert also questioned confident claims and acronym density. This is a reported demonstration, not an estimate of system-wide fraud rates.

Application: verify existence and support separately, inspect figures, retain provenance, and distinguish readable prose from valid research. The preprint criticism describes IEEE Access's assessment in this case; it does not establish a universal quota. The demonstrator's removal of a disclosure is a cautionary example, not a workflow to imitate.

## Integrity, scale and provenance links

- [STM Integrity Hub](https://stm-assoc.org/what-we-do/strategic-areas/research-integrity/integrity-hub/): official overview inspected. Publisher screening infrastructure supplies warning signals and integrations; screening does not replace evaluating the research.
- [Molecular Connections](https://molecularconnections.com/): official vendor overview inspected. Identifies the second checking provider; no public independent accuracy assessment was established here.
- [NCSES, Publications Output: U.S. Trends and International Comparisons](https://ncses.nsf.gov/pubs/nsb202333): official report page inspected. Provides bibliometric scale context. Counts of publications are not direct counts of reviewer workload or fraud.
- [PubPeer](https://pubpeer.com/) and [Retraction Watch](https://retractionwatch.com/): service sites inspected as routes to post-publication discussion and updates. Check a specific claim against the paper and publisher's record; neither a comment nor the absence of a listing settles validity.
- [An expert criticism on post-publication peer review platforms: the case of pubpeer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40199-025-00580-z): publisher record inspected, including a 19 November 2025 editorial notice of concerns and investigation. Spectrum discusses it as an example involving an allegedly fabricated reference. This skill does not independently adjudicate that allegation or use the paper as integrity guidance.
- [Southampton CChES electronic lab notebooks](https://cches.co.uk/electronic-lab-notebooks/): institutional account inspected. A practical provenance example, not a universal requirement for a particular notebook product. The Max Planck sample-ID example in Spectrum had no direct source link; it is not treated as independently verified here. Blockchain is not required by this procedure.

## Supplementary official guidance

These are additional sources, not claims that the Spectrum article cited them:

- [IEEE AI author guidelines](https://open.ieee.org/author-guidelines-for-artificial-intelligence-ai-generated-text/): generated content requires disclosure identifying the system, affected portions and use; editing/grammar assistance is distinguished, with disclosure recommended. Recheck the target publication's current rules.
- [IEEE, Protecting Research Integrity: Best Practices for Authors Using AI-Assisted Research](https://open.ieee.org/protecting-research-integrity-best-practices-for-authors-using-ai-assisted-research/): supports original-source verification, checking current versions and comparing AI summaries against source texts. Its stylistic cues should not be treated as authorship proof.
- [COPE, Authorship and AI tools](https://doi.org/10.24318/cCVRZBms): AI cannot assume authorship accountability; authors remain responsible and should transparently describe applicable AI use.
- [AIP Publishing AI policy](https://publishing.aip.org/resources/researchers/policies-and-ethics/ai-policy/): emphasizes verifying generated content and references and retaining original data/images. Its reviewer confidentiality restrictions are venue-specific; check the policy before processing a confidential review manuscript.

The portable workflow and humanizer contract are editorial/engineering recommendations synthesized from these sources, not a claim that any publisher mandates this exact sequence. Refresh policies and source versions when applying them to a real submission.
