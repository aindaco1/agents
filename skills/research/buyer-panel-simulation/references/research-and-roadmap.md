# What to borrow from Simile for market research

For a first study, pick a decision someone will make soon, with two assets and a measurable next step. A sales team might need to choose between asking prospects to book a demo and offering a short technical review. A marketing team might need to decide which rental offer to send to its subscribers. In both cases, a modest number of well-described buyers can expose objections worth testing. The value of adding more simulated buyers depends on whether the system can recover differences that real buyers exhibited.

## Findings that change the design

| Source | What was measured | Consequence for this skill |
|---|---|---|
| Park et al., *Social Simulacra* (2022) and *Generative Agents* (2023) | Designers prototyped populated communities. Smallville added memory, reflection and planning to a 25-agent town and evaluated behavioral believability. | Preserve the buying situation. Add conversation or memory when the decision depends on a previous interaction, such as a champion taking an objection to procurement. Believability alone supplies no sales benchmark. [Social Simulacra](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.04024) · [Generative Agents, evaluation and limitations](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.03442) |
| Park et al., interview-grounded agents | The original 1,052-person study reported GSS agreement at 85% of participants' two-week self-agreement. The revision reports 60.4% raw agreement from demographics, 69.4% from interviews, 72.8% from combined interviews and surveys, and 79.5% human retest agreement. Richer inputs improved some tasks and left incentivized economic choices unimproved. | Build cases from evidence about what buyers did, could approve, and gave up. Track stated intent and paid behavior as separate endpoints. [Original study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.10109v1) · [Revised analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.10109) |
| *Finetuning LLMs for Human Behavior Prediction in Social Science Experiments* (EMNLP 2025) | SocSci210 contains 2.9 million responses across 210 experiments. The authors compare response distributions in entirely unseen studies and in new conditions within a familiar study. | Hold out whole campaigns. A new subject line from a campaign already used for tuning is a weaker test than a new campaign. Revisit training once there is a collection of comparable experiments. [EMNLP paper](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1530/) |
| Maier et al., semantic similarity rating | The authors elicited free text and mapped it to scale anchors. Across 57 personal-care concept surveys with 9,300 human responses, relative concept rankings and response distributions compared favorably to direct numeric prompting. Anchor choice and domain affected the result. | Keep a buyer's reason alongside the action. Consider SSR if the human benchmark actually asks a Likert purchase-intent question. The current skill uses a discrete next step. [Paper and limitations](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.08338v3) |
| Pricing experiments with real financial incentives | Four product studies compared model and human willingness to pay. Synthetic answers overstated willingness for two hedonic goods and showed mixed errors for two utilitarian goods. Even plausible average prices could lead to different profit decisions. | Test real checkout or signed-order behavior before adopting a price. Include the option to walk away and record who controls the budget. [Journal of Marketing Analytics study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1057/s41270-026-00542-7) |
| *Synthetic Data in Marketing Research* (2026 preprint) | Aggregate agreement on attitude questions could conceal poor differentiation between respondents. | Examine which buyer cases change their minds and check errors by segment whenever the human sample permits it. [Marketing evaluation paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13995) |

Simile describes a commercial population grounded in proprietary human data and recurring evaluations. Its confidence model learns from the error between previous predictions and human observations. In its engineering write-up, an entire experimental sample stays together when splitting evaluation folds. A portable prompt or a panel's internal agreement provides no equivalent accuracy estimate. This skill reports uncertainty from unresolved cases and can compare a frozen result with observed outcomes. [Simile](https://www.simile.com/) · [Confidence methodology](https://www.simile.com/blog/confidence)

## Open-source projects and the build choice

| Project | What to reuse or investigate | When it earns the extra dependency |
|---|---|---|
| Expected Parrot EDSL | Questions, scenarios, agents and models are composable survey components. The same instrument can run across different models. | First candidate for batch execution once the local studies need many questions, variants, or model comparisons. Keep the study and response files as an interchange format. [Repository and examples](https://github.com/expectedparrot/edsl) |
| Microsoft TinyTroupe | Its bottled-gazpacho example compares simulated answers with a human survey. Its focus group demonstrates interaction between respondents. | Reproduce the survey comparison before experimenting with group discussions. For B2B, first collect independent judgments so a simulated committee does not amplify the first speaker. [Repository and validation notebooks](https://github.com/microsoft/TinyTroupe) |
| PyMC Labs semantic-similarity-rating | The authors publish the text-to-scale scoring implementation. | Try it against an existing human concept tracker using the same question and anchors. Check rankings *and* distribution errors on unseen concepts before adding it. [Authors' implementation](https://github.com/pymc-labs/semantic-similarity-rating) |
| Google DeepMind Concordia | A game master can maintain shared state and resolve what one actor observes after another acts. | Useful when a seller, champion, finance lead and procurement team have sequential choices that affect the next turn. A simple email comparison has no such dependency. [Repository and execution model](https://github.com/google-deepmind/concordia) |

The public-source workflow also draws on two agent skills:

- Corey Haines’s customer-research skill searches the places where buyers already discuss alternatives and distinguishes evidence strength and recency. This skill applies that approach to buyer-authored records and asks the researcher to trace a review or testimonial back to its original account. [Customer Research](https://github.com/coreyhaines31/marketingskills/blob/main/skills/customer-research/SKILL.md) · [Source guides](https://github.com/coreyhaines31/marketingskills/blob/main/skills/customer-research/references/source-guides.md)
- Bayram Annakov’s synthetic-market-research skill reuses a persona set across concept variants and maps free-text reactions to purchase-intent ratings. That is an option for a study with a matching human intent scale. Its generated personas remain illustrative when no buyer evidence exists. An observed sale or qualified reply needs a benchmark that measures that action. [Synthetic Market Research](https://github.com/BayramAnnakov/synthetic-market-research/blob/master/SKILL.md)

For broad searches, LangChain's Open Deep Research agent offers a reference implementation for scoping a research brief, searching and distilling sources. This skill uses the host's existing web search and a [source comparison table](public-comparables.md); adding a search service is optional. [Open Deep Research](https://github.com/langchain-ai/open_deep_research)

The initial implementation has a small, provider-independent packet and report format plus an optional on-device model runner. These repositories informed the design. None is a runtime dependency of the packet or scoring tools.

## A practical sequence

1. **Choose the first decision.** One audience, one channel, one next step, incumbent plus challenger. Use several distinct buying situations covering willing, unwilling and uncertain cases. For outreach, retain a loss, an unanswered inquiry and a buyer who needed someone else's sign-off. Record the original text, eligibility and decision cost.
2. **Replay a completed comparison blind.** A custodian keeps the historical outcome aside. Build the cases from information available before the campaign. Lock the synthetic result, then reveal eligible and responding accounts per arm. Compare the sign and size of the difference against the zero-change baseline, plus a pre-existing segment or campaign base-rate predictor if available. Use absolute percentage-point error; inspect weak segments, reversals, ambiguous cases and the sampling uncertainty of the human result. If the historical evidence was observational, describe an association.
3. **Attempt a fresh replication.** Predeclare a business-relevant error tolerance, the minimum effect worth acting on and a sample plan using the observed baseline. Randomize the actual exposure by account or buyer, and measure the original endpoint within its original time window. Decide after the human result whether this audience and question merit routine use. A handful of examples can expose a failure; repeated independent campaigns are needed to estimate typical error.
4. **Expand only where the comparison improves.** Try structured surveys through EDSL for volume, SSR for a validated intent scale, or TinyTroupe/Concordia if interaction is itself the hypothesis. Compare each addition to the simpler version on the same held-out data. Track recurring misses when the product, channel or model changes. Use real funnel measurements for projections of revenue, win rate or market size.
