# Academic prose: preserve the science

Use with the humanizer's ordinary two-pass limit. This mode applies across disciplines and languages; clarity does not require colloquial English, synthetic personality, or a fixed sentence rhythm.

## Preservation anchors

Before editing, identify:

- Claims and their epistemic status: observation, association, causal inference, interpretation, hypothesis, conjecture, or proven statement.
- Population, setting, time period, comparators, assumptions, exclusions and limitations.
- Numbers, units, sample sizes, denominators, intervals, p-values, equations, notation, and technical terms.
- Citation keys, quotations, edition/page/archival locators, preprint labels, correction notices, and data/code links.
- Authorship/contribution statements, methods provenance, funding/conflicts, ethics statements, and AI-use disclosures.

In a faithful edit, preserve these. If one looks wrong, flag it for evidence checking instead of silently repairing it from model memory. A requested substantive correction belongs in the research workflow and requires source verification.

## Edit for a scholarly reader

1. Remove inflated importance, generic opening claims, redundant summaries, and formulaic transitions only where they obstruct the argument.
2. Reduce acronym load by spelling out or removing genuinely unnecessary abbreviations. Keep standard abbreviations, precise definitions, and consistent notation.
3. Preserve established technical names and useful repetition. Unusual synonym substitutions can corrupt the concept; do not paraphrase a defined construct merely to vary the vocabulary.
4. Distinguish filler hedges from scientific qualification. Words such as “may,” “under these assumptions,” and “in this sample” can carry the central limitation. Do not replace “was associated with” with “caused,” or “did not detect an effect” with “proved no effect.”
5. Keep appropriate disciplinary form, including passive voice, conventional headings, careful repetition and restrained tone when useful. Do not import creative-writing advice about scenes, nonlinear movement, fragments, or process residue into a methods section.
6. Check abstract and conclusion against the body after shortening them. Do not broaden the population, strengthen novelty, remove contrary evidence, or promote exploratory results to confirmed findings.

## Keep editing and verification distinct

Real-looking citations, fluent prose, and clean figures do not establish trustworthy research. Flag unverifiable references, unsupported certainty, inconsistent quantities, and nonsensical figure labels. Do not invent missing citations or redraw a result to look plausible. For evidence repair or full manuscript development, return to `research-paper-authoring`.

Preserve AI provenance during every pass. Never remove an acknowledgment or alter authorship to imply research was performed by someone else. Follow the current venue's disclosure requirements; a stylistic cleanup does not change the underlying writing process.

Compare the revision with its anchors before returning it. Example of an acceptable edit: shorten “The findings of this investigation suggest that X may improve Y in this sample” to “These findings suggest X may improve Y in this sample.” The uncertainty and scope remain. If the source is scientifically unsound, provide the faithful edit only with a separate, concise unresolved-issue note when that issue materially affects use.

## Why these constraints exist

- [Benjamin Skuse's IEEE Spectrum investigation](https://spectrum.ieee.org/ai-generated-scientific-papers) illustrates how fluent manuscripts can retain citation and figure defects. Its style observations are contextual editing clues, not an authorship test.
- [Topaz and Bahl's PNAS critique](https://www.pnas.org/doi/full/10.1073/pnas.2620928123) disputes converting lexical shifts into calibrated article-level AI prevalence. Avoid blacklists of “AI words” and preserve ordinary or non-native scholarly language.
- [He and Bu's reply on journal AI policies](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2617762123) distinguishes aggregate writing signals from individual misconduct and acknowledges that the measure cannot separate polishing from substantive generation.
- [IEEE's AI author guidance](https://open.ieee.org/author-guidelines-for-artificial-intelligence-ai-generated-text/) distinguishes generated content from editing/grammar assistance. Check the target venue's current rule rather than imposing a universal disclosure exemption.
