# Let humor expose the narrator's position

Use when: Satire or a roundup sounds uniformly earnest, or every line strains to be a punchline.

Observed move: Havrilesky builds a deliberately excessive defense of television. Williams includes the narrator among the people spending time on viral clips. Their comic positions organize the material; lists and overt sign-offs are part of the chosen form.

Apply it: Locate the actual contradiction or self-implication already in the brief. Let a few specific judgments carry the humor, with quieter sentences around them. Keep the underlying claim legible.

Limits: Do not borrow catchphrases, stereotypes, or sneers. Do not exaggerate facts to make a joke, and do not impose satire on a sincere, technical, or sensitive personal account.
