# Replace an unhelpful abstraction with a precise distinction

Use when: A response discusses a large social problem while leaving the writer's immediate question untouched.

Observed move: The pregnancy column distinguishes regret about a promise from apology for the pregnancy itself. The relationships column separates a sweeping social diagnosis from particular rejected encounters. Its aggressive imagined framing is a counterexample, not a requirement.

Apply it: Find the distinction already supported by the facts. State what it changes about the immediate action or wording. For an editing task, use only the actions and claims already authorized in the draft.

Limits: Do not replace ambiguity with your own theory, invent dialogue, or give outdated employment or medical guidance. A sharp distinction is useful only when the evidence supports it.
