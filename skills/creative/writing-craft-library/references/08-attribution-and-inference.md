# Separate the evidence from the writer's inference

Use when: An argument relies on a source's wording, but the evidence and interpretation blur.

Observed move: The trade column places attributed industry statements beside the writer's interpretation. The offshoring column makes a word-substitution table available for inspection rather than only announcing that its language is misleading.

Apply it: Keep quotation, paraphrase, and inference distinct. Where the supplied source includes a revealing detail or comparison, let the reader see it before the interpretation. Preserve speaker and citation identity.

Limits: Do not adopt the columnist's certainty about motives or import historical facts into new work. Exact quotations and technical terms cannot be adjusted to strengthen a rhetorical point.
