# Follow an answer and keep speakers distinct

Use when: An interview becomes a string of generic questions or edited replies all sound alike.

Observed move: Havrilesky asks follow-ups that emerge from the subject's previous answer. In the Ramis/Cusack conversation, the subjects qualify and tease one another; their turns do not perform interchangeable jobs.

Apply it: For question preparation, ask about a concrete surprise or claim in the supplied answer. For editing, preserve attribution, intent, register, and meaningful hesitation. Keep an interviewer's framing distinct from a subject's words.

Limits: Never invent an answer or turn a paraphrase into a direct quote. Do not treat interviewees' language as evidence of the interviewer's prose style.
