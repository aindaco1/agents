| Choice in the notes | Transferable editorial move |
|---|---|
| Introduce other Queens in the background before Andy notices them. | Let the audience discover visual evidence over time; a later recognition can reframe earlier images. |
| Keep the hallway kiss's identity uncertain. | Distinguish a character's assumption from what the camera actually confirms. |
| Use repeated pours with changing Queens in the same frame. | Repetition and variation can convey time, intoxication, subjective experience, and a social pattern simultaneously. |
| Connect the phone, bedroom mirror, overlapping reflections, and shattered mirror. | Develop an existing family of images into layered storytelling rather than importing unrelated symbols. |
| Keep Andy visible while Benny fixes his hair and discusses Alana. | Composition and simultaneous behavior can reveal competing priorities without diagnosis. |
| Shorten the kitchen history and retain the Coke bottle beat that makes the women face each other. | Preserve physical staging that creates an encounter while cutting explanation around it. |
| Let Benny repeat gestures or compliments with different women. | Expose a pattern through behavior and recognition; do not require another character to explain him. |
| Consider matched framing before and after sex. | An ellipsis and a visual relationship between shots can create comedy and save production effort. |
| Let Andy and Alana observe, recognize, and look at each other before speaking. | Give discovery and reaction room; cut the subsequent verbal recap. |
| Make leaving an active refusal and preserve the final cigarette gesture echoing the earlier raised cup. | End through action and transformed repetition rather than a speech summarizing growth. |

The notes also preserve successful comic business and a possible racialized layer in Benny's behavior, conditional on the filmmaker's intent. This is a useful model for economy that does not flatten complexity. The proposed secret recording and musical reuse is explicitly an adventurous new idea, not a claim about the existing screenplay.

Tone: brief overall judgment; scene-specific notes; concrete images; candid enthusiasm for successful details; proposed alternatives phrased as experiments. Avoid imitating every colloquial expression. Borrow the specificity and relationship to the filmmaker.
