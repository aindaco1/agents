# Applying the lens to a rough cut

Use only when reviewing footage or helping interpret existing cut notes. Keep the evidence boundary explicit: a transcript cannot establish composition, performance, continuity, musical timing, or the effect of a cut. A few stills can support observations about those frames, not a verdict on the whole sequence. If playback is unavailable, review the supplied notes and identify what needs viewing.

Identify the cut/version and the timecode basis. Watch and listen through before making a final judgment, then revisit the relevant moments. Do not call a change an improvement over an earlier version unless that version was reviewed or the comparison is attributed to the supplied notes. State any material playback limitations.

## What to attend to

- **Information and reaction:** Does the viewer see the action, recognition, and consequence needed to read the beat? A missing glance, movement, or spatial relation can matter more than dialogue. Consider when the audience knows something relative to the character.
- **Composition and staging:** Can the important gesture, prop, costume detail, or reaction be seen? Would another available angle, a restrained crop, or a different in/out point clarify it? Leave distracting background behavior only when it contributes.
- **Montage and rhythm:** What meaning arises between shots? Check order, repetition, contrast, duration, ellipsis, and anticipation. Judge a hold by what it accumulates. Avoid equating fast cutting with energy or long takes with poetry.
- **Sound, music, and silence:** Listen for rhythmic motivation, musical phrases, abrupt edits, sound bridges, perspective, and useful contradiction with the image. Distinguish a deliberately abrasive transition from an accidental discontinuity. Do not prescribe constant score or effects to explain every action.
- **Performance and comedy:** Preserve the setup, perceptible action, reaction, and necessary breath. A stronger reaction or an extra beat may improve a shorter sequence. Cut accidental camera looks or exposed construction when they undermine the intended form.
- **Continuity:** Check the geography, actions, wardrobe, props, eyelines, and audiovisual matches that affect understanding or attention. Distinguish intentional discontinuity from an error. A strong final image may still need a practical continuity solution.

## Suggest changes appropriate to the stage

Prefer edits, alternate takes, existing reactions, in/out adjustments, crops, or sound work when the evidence supports them. Do not claim coverage exists without seeing it or having a reliable inventory. Label suggestions as conditional when necessary. Separate an edit experiment from a pickup, reshoot, new performance, or uncertain visual effect.

Do not casually prescribe alterations that change a performer's apparent nudity or performance. Describe the continuity problem and assess alternatives within the production's established consent and available material.

Deliver a short overall judgment and the consequential notes in a useful order. Use verified timestamps and plain descriptions of the moment. Each note should explain the perceived effect and an actionable experiment; avoid converting every observation into an obligation. Name what should remain untouched. Recommend a post-production handoff only when the requested scope and actual evidence support it.
